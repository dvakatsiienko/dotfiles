# the diorama — story bible

one t-rex, one valley, one day. three repos show three hours of that day. every image has a
light and a dark version, and **light is always day, dark is always night** — the theme switch is
the clock.

## the tale — «short arms, big workshop»

Oles lives at the bottom of Pinefold, a valley of pines, a slow creek, and a castle town on the far
hill called Lanternhill. He is a tyrannosaurus, which is less useful than it sounds. His head is
the size of a wheelbarrow and his arms can barely reach his own scarf.

So Oles builds things. Pulleys to lift the kettle. A bench low enough for his chin. An organ
whose keys he plays with a row of wooden levers, because fingers were never the plan. The cabin is
full of these: small machines that let small arms do big work.

One spring the creek brought him a brass lamp, green with moss. He polished it with his tail,
because that was what reached, and a small lavender smoke curled out and yawned. The djinni's
name was Dym. Dym grants no big wishes. He grants the small, exact ones — «sort the shelf»,
«fix the gate», «count the logs» — and for each one he calls a helper out of the lamp's light:
an owl who plans the day, a bulldog who builds, a basset who looks every job over, a dalmatian who
checks it twice. Oles calls them the crew. The crew calls him boss, which he dislikes.

Every morning Oles walks the creek path to Lanternhill. A wren rides on his head the whole way;
nobody remembers when that started. The town is small and full of fireflies, and every firefly
keeps a shop: one sells trophies, one sells star charts and rocket tickets, one keeps ledgers,
one writes letters for aliens who drop by, one prints calling cards. Oles buys what the workshop
needs, carries the basket on his tail, and every shopkeeper gives him too much change because
counting it out for him feels rude.

In the evening he walks home. The fireflies light the path to the treeline, and one of them
always comes along in a paper lantern, just to see the cabin. At home Dym wakes up, the crew
comes out, and the workshop hums until the fire goes low. Then Oles sleeps on the big round rug,
and the machines stay quiet until the wren knocks on the window at dawn.

Nothing in Pinefold is urgent. That is the whole point of it.

## the day, mapped to the repos

- **profile** (`dvakatsiienko`) — **home, settled.** Oles by his cabin: the kettle over the fire
  circle beside the cabin, the garden, the well, the scarf drying on the line, Lanternhill small on the far hill.
  - night: the fire lit, the scarf on him now, fireflies over the meadow.
  - tour tiles: frame = the lamp on the workbench · bytes = the shop sign and a firefly lantern.
- **bytes** — **midday, the market.** A Lanternhill street, shop fronts in a row, Oles with the
  basket on his tail reading a shopping list the wren holds for him.
  - night: shutters down, every sign lit by its own firefly, the street empty.
  - app rows: each app is **its shop sign** on an iron bracket. The sign board is the app icon,
    drawn big; the bracket is thin. The container never outweighs the icon.
- **frame** — **evening, the workshop.** The cabin cut open like a dollhouse, one room per
  readme section:
  - fleet — Dym above the lamp, the crew at their desks
  - chords — the lever organ; Oles plays it with his chin and tail
  - mirror — a tall mirror; what stands in front of it stands inside it too
  - machine — the pulley wall, the kettle lift, the brew barrel
  - link it — the front door, a key on a hook
  - night: the same room by lamp and firelight, Dym glowing, the crew awake.

## connectors — the stash

scenes that link one place to another rather than showing a place settled. they live in
`out/stash/` until a story event needs them.

- **the walk** — the creek path from the cabin to Lanternhill; by night the walk home with a
  firefly lantern. fits a «went to the store and brought something back» beat.

## tone

- calm, quiet, a little magic and a little mysterious. cartoonish, but not a happy land: nobody
  smiles at the camera, nothing is cheerful on purpose.
- never a battle, never a rush, never a roar.
- humour is dry: the short arms, and helpers doing small jobs very seriously.
- flowers are an accent. ferns and foliage carry the green; a scene holds a few blooms, not a field.
- magic is small and soft: a lavender curl of smoke, a firefly glow. never beams, never sparkle
  storms.

## names

- Oles — the t-rex. The name comes from «ліс», forest.
- Dym — the djinni, «smoke».
- Pinefold — the valley. Lanternhill — the town.
- the crew — owl (plans), bulldog (builds), basset (reviews), dalmatian (verifies).
- the wren — has no name, does not need one.
