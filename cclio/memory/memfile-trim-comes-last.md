**Dima's ruling: the «why» paragraphs in memfiles are MANDATORY while cclio is being built.**
Do not cut them for token weight, and do not let [DOT-198](linear://linear.app/issue/DOT-198)'s
25k target pull the trim forward.

**Why they earn their length right now:** cclio is a half-migrated agent reasoning about rules
written by another one. A rule without its reason cannot be re-aimed — it can only be obeyed or
deleted. The whys are what let a leaf be corrected instead of guessed at. Once the surface is
settled, that scaffolding has done its job and becomes the clutter DOT-73 describes.

**The sequence before any trim, in his order — do not reorder it:**

1. cclio's own story finishes (the DOT-188 trial reaches a verdict Dima gives).
2. the postponed obsidian `worklog.md` and `inbox.md` are exhausted — see
   [[obsidian-inbox-protocol]]; both are frozen by him today.
3. frozen handoffs are reviewed, resolved and taken care of.
4. **then** the memory is freed from clutter.

📌 The tension this leaf resolves, so nobody re-litigates it: DOT-73's body records that opus
writes ~70% unneeded text everywhere, and that is true of these leaves. It is accepted cost for
now, not an oversight.

🚨 **correction: the spec is NOT cut off. It is complete, and it was misread.** An earlier version
of this leaf claimed DOT-73's note broke mid-sentence at «info that», and made «ask Dima to finish
it» a precondition for any trim. The bullet simply continued onto its own line, and reads in full:

> what is needed in skills, rules, your mem files: **info that is useful to you, in a format
> appropriate to you**

That is the whole spec and it is enough to trim against. **The blocker it invented is removed** —
what gates the trim is the sequence above, nothing else.

then blocked real work. When a quote looks incomplete, re-read the original before writing the gap
into a rule.
